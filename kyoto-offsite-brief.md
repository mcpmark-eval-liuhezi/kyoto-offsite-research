## 1. Dinner options in Gion
**Tracked in:** [Issue #1 — Dinner options in Gion](https://github.com/mcpmark-eval-liuhezi/kyoto-offsite-research/issues/1)

Gion (eastern central Kyoto, between the Kamogawa River and Yasaka Shrine) offers everything from two-star kaiseki ryotei to casual izakaya. Highlights of the research:

- **Gion Maruyama (祇園丸山)** — two MICHELIN stars (2026 guide), kaiseki ryotei established 1988; reservation-only via MICHELIN Guide / TABLEALL / TableCheck / Pocket Concierge. Private rooms on the 2nd floor suit a group.
- **Gion Shinbashi Nakatani (祇園新橋なか谷)** — kappo/kaiseki at 59-1 Motoyoshicho, Higashiyama-ku; 4.8/5 on Tripadvisor; dinner from 6:00 PM; ~¥11,000 average; Awaji wild fish + Kyoto vegetables.
- **Izuju (いづ重)** — late-Meiji-era Kyoto-style sushi in front of Yasaka Shrine, 292-1 Gionmachi Kitagawa; 4.2/5 on Tripadvisor; famous saba-zushi; opens 10:30.
- **Bungo Gion (豊後 祇園)** — opened 2023 on Shinbashi-dori; Oita Wagyu courses grilled tableside.
- **Gion Nanba (祇園 なんば)** — Michelin-starred history (2010, 2013); 279-7 Gionmachi Kitagawa; book well ahead.

**Takeaways:** Book kaiseki weeks ahead; mix one splurge night with a casual/interactive night; expect overtourism queues and be respectful of the residential lanes (no photographing geiko/maiko).

## 2. Quick lunches near Kyoto Station
**Tracked in:** [Issue #2 — Quick lunches near Kyoto Station](https://github.com/mcpmark-eval-liuhezi/kyoto-offsite-research/issues/2)

The station is a dining hub — know your zone (Porta B1–B2, Miyako Michi, Asty Road, Isetan, Kyoto Ramen Koji on 10F). Highlights:

- **Kyoto Ramen Koji (京都ラーメン小路, "Kyoto Ramen Street")** — 10th floor of the station building, south of the Grand Stairway; regional ramen brands; quick, cheap, no reservation needed. The safest bet for a group on a schedule.
- **Porta underground mall** — B1/B2 Karasuma side; food-court variety (Japanese, Italian, Korean, cafés) at cheap prices, all-weather.
- **Honke Daiichi Asahi (本家第一旭), main branch** — ~5-min walk from the station at Takabashi; open roughly 6:00 a.m.–1:00 a.m.; ~¥800–850 signature ramen; expect a queue that moves fast.
- **Wagyu Beef Ramen (Kyoto Station Restaurant)** — 2F, 3-4 Higashikujo Kitakarasumacho, Minami-ku; halal-friendly; +81 80-2800-2880; open daily with late-night hours.
- **isoism** — Kyoto-vegetable cuisine near the station; lunch seatings 11:30/12:00/13:00/13:30.
- **Kamado-Takitate-Gohan Doi (Hachijo Exit)** — kamado rice + Kyoto pickles, plus souvenirs.
- Short hop away: **Menbaka Fire Ramen** (757-2 Minamiiseyacho, Kamigyo-ku; 4.5/5 from 1,230 reviews; 11:00–21:00 daily; reservation-only; vegan/vegetarian/gluten-free/halal-friendly), **Sushizen** (41-2 Koromonotanacho, Nakagyo-ku; Michelin Bib Gourmand; 19 seats; 10:00–18:00), **Wajoryomen Sugari** (471-1 Kannondocho, Nakagyo-ku; tsukemen; 4.5/5; 11:30–15:00 / 18:00–22:00).

**Takeaways:** Ramen Koji and Porta for speed; Daiichi Asahi for the classic experience (queue 20–30 min, go off-peak); Menbaka for a memorable team lunch if reserved ahead.

**Digest — the overall picture for a March visitor:**
Kyoto is riding a record national tourism boom (42.7M visitors last year, Japan ranked the world's top travel destination), and it's one of the country's most-crowded hotspots as a result. The city and national government are responding with crowd-management and pricing experiments — possible higher bus fares for travellers, a broader debate about charging foreign visitors more, and public etiquette campaigns (Fushimi Inari's pushback on disrespectful filming/dancing). Practically, that means: book accommodation and group dinners early, budget for possible visitor-facing price increases, lean on the subway rather than buses at rush hour, and keep shrine etiquette in mind. For March specifically, the big variable is sakura — Kyoto's blossoms typically open late March to early April (they hit March 25 in 2026, a week early), so expect peak-season crowds and premium prices in the last third of the month, and plan early-morning starts for headline temples. On the upside, the city keeps adding fresh draws — the free Ghibli Kyoto map at 7-Elevens, the "Zen and Ghibli" exhibition, and the Nintendo Museum's new Super Family Restaurant — which make great team activities if timed-entry tickets are reserved ahead. Net-net: a spectacular but busy and increasingly managed destination — plan early, travel early, and expect costs to be the main moving target.
